# Demo Script

> A demo is not a feature tour. It is a story about how your solution solves the customer's problem. Every click should have a reason. Every transition should connect to a business outcome.

---

## Opening (Set the Business Context)

*Duration: 2-3 minutes. Do NOT touch the keyboard yet.*

### What to Say

> *[e.g., "Before I show you anything, let me remind us why we're here. During our discovery session, we learned that your CS team spends 75 hours a week manually tracking shipments across three systems. Your NPS has dropped from 58 to 32. And your largest account -- $18 million in annual revenue -- has told you they're evaluating alternatives.*
>
> *What I'm going to show you today is a working proof of concept that addresses the core of this problem: getting your CS reps instant access to real-time shipment status. This is built with your live data -- these are real shipments, real statuses, real locations.*
>
> *I'll walk you through two scenarios that your team faces every day. Ready?"]*

### Checklist Before You Start

- [ ] Screen is clean -- no notifications, no extra tabs, no personal bookmarks
- [ ] Demo environment is loaded and tested within the last hour
- [ ] Browser is zoomed to a comfortable reading level (125-150%)
- [ ] You know the names/tracking numbers of 3 demo shipments that show different statuses
- [ ] Backup plan is ready (see Backup Plan section below)

---

## Demo Flow

### Step 1: The "Where Is My Shipment?" Scenario

**Duration:** *[e.g., "3-4 minutes"]*

**What to say:**
> *[e.g., "Let's start with the most common scenario: a customer calls and says, 'Where is my shipment? The tracking number is [NUMBER].' Today, your rep would open SAP, then check the carrier portal, then maybe call the warehouse. That takes 15 minutes on average. Watch what happens now."]*

**What to show:**
- *[e.g., "Type tracking number into search bar"]*
- *[e.g., "Show the result appearing in under 2 seconds"]*
- *[e.g., "Point out: current location, status, estimated arrival, full history timeline"]*

**Key talking point:**
> *[e.g., "That took 2 seconds. Not 15 minutes. Two seconds. And the rep never left this screen."]*

**Pause here.** *Let the audience react. Do not rush to the next feature.*

---

### Step 2: The Dashboard Overview

**Duration:** *[e.g., "2-3 minutes"]*

**What to say:**
> *[e.g., "Now let me show you what your operations manager sees. Instead of compiling a spreadsheet every morning, they open this dashboard and get a live view of every active shipment."]*

**What to show:**
- *[e.g., "Navigate to the main dashboard view"]*
- *[e.g., "Show total active shipments, on-time percentage, delayed count"]*
- *[e.g., "Filter by status (delayed, in transit, delivered)"]*
- *[e.g., "Click into a delayed shipment to show detail"]*

**Key talking point:**
> *[e.g., "Notice the 'delayed' count. Right now it shows [X] shipments. Your team would see this before the customer calls, not after."]*

---

### Step 3: Proactive Notification

**Duration:** *[e.g., "2-3 minutes"]*

**What to say:**
> *[e.g., "Here's where it gets really interesting. When the system detects a delay, it doesn't wait for the customer to call. Let me show you what happens."]*

**What to show:**
- *[e.g., "Show a shipment that triggered a delay notification"]*
- *[e.g., "Open the notification email that was sent automatically"]*
- *[e.g., "Point out the timing: delay detected at [time], email sent at [time + 3 min]"]*

**Key talking point:**
> *[e.g., "The customer got this email before they even knew there was a problem. That is the difference between reactive and proactive service. That is how you go from an NPS of 32 to 50+."]*

## Key Transitions

*Transitions connect features to business outcomes. Never just say "and next I'll show you..." Instead:*

| From                | To                    | Transition Language                                     |
|---------------------|-----------------------|---------------------------------------------------------|
| *Opening*           | *Step 1 (Search)*     | *"Let me show you what this looks like in practice..."* |
| *Step 1 (Search)*   | *Step 2 (Dashboard)*  | *[e.g., "That was the rep's view. Now let me show you what your ops manager sees..."]*  |
| *Step 2 (Dashboard)* | *Step 3 (Notifications)* | *[e.g., "Seeing the data is powerful. But what if you could act on it automatically?"]*  |
| *Step 3*            | *Closing*             | *[e.g., "So that's the core of what we've built in [X] weeks. Let me put it in context."]*  |

## Closing (Next Steps)

**Duration:** *2 minutes. Stop sharing your screen. Make eye contact (or look at the camera).*

### What to Say

> *[e.g., "Let me summarize what you just saw. In three weeks, we built a working system that:*
> - *Reduces shipment lookup from 15 minutes to 2 seconds*
> - *Gives your ops team real-time visibility across all shipments*
> - *Proactively notifies customers about delays before they call*
>
> *This is a proof of concept, not a finished product. But it proves that the approach works with your real data. The question now is: what do we do next?*
>
> *I'd recommend a 4-week production build that adds [key items] and rolls this out to your full CS team. I can have a detailed proposal to you by [date].*
>
> *What questions do you have?"]*

### Do NOT

- Do not apologize for what is missing ("sorry, we didn't have time for...")
- Do not show features that are half-built
- Do not keep demoing past the planned stopping point
- Do not answer long technical questions during the demo -- note them and follow up

## Backup Plan (If Demo Breaks)

*Something will break eventually. Plan for it.*

### Pre-Demo Preparation

- [ ] Take screenshots of every key screen in case you need to present statically
- [ ] Record a video walkthrough as a backup
- [ ] Have a local/offline version if the live environment depends on internet
- [ ] Test the demo end-to-end within 1 hour of the actual demo

### If Something Breaks During the Demo

**Step 1:** Do not panic. Do not say "oh no." Pause for one second.

**Step 2:** Say one of these:
> - "Let me switch to another example while that loads."
> - "Interesting -- that's the kind of edge case we're still hardening. Let me show you the expected result." *(show screenshot)*
> - "The live environment is being temperamental. Let me show you the recorded walkthrough instead."

**Step 3:** Move on. Do not debug live. Do not explain the error. Fix it after the meeting.

### If the Entire Environment Is Down

> "I prepared a recorded walkthrough for exactly this situation. Let me share that instead, and we can do a live follow-up session later this week."

*Play the pre-recorded video. Narrate over it as if it were live.*
