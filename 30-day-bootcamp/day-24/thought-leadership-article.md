## Hook

*Write a compelling opening that grabs attention. Start with a surprising statistic, a bold claim, or a vivid anecdote.*

*Your first paragraph should make the reader stop scrolling. Aim for 3-5 sentences that establish urgency and relevance.*

## The Problem

*Define the problem your article addresses. Why should readers care? Use data to quantify the impact.*

*Consider including:*
- *A specific metric or data point that illustrates the severity*
- *Who is affected and how*
- *What happens if the problem goes unaddressed*

---

## The Conventional Wisdom (and Why It's Wrong)

*What does the industry currently believe? Why is this thinking outdated or incomplete?*

*Articulate the dominant approach clearly and fairly before dismantling it. Readers respect writers who understand the opposing view before challenging it.*

*Key points to address:*
- *What is the prevailing approach or belief?*
- *Where did it come from and why did it make sense at the time?*
- *What has changed that makes it insufficient now?*

---

## A Better Approach

*Present your thesis. What should the industry do instead? Ground this in your FDE experience and technical expertise.*

*This is the core of your article. Be specific and actionable. Draw on real patterns you have observed working directly with customers and deploying solutions in the field.*

*Structure your argument:*
- *State your thesis in one clear sentence*
- *Explain the reasoning behind it*
- *Connect it to broader trends in the industry*

---

## Evidence and Examples

*Provide a concrete example, case study, or data point that supports your argument. This is where your credibility comes from.*

*Guidelines:*
- *Anonymize customer details if necessary*
- *Include before/after metrics where possible*
- *Walk through the implementation at a level your audience can follow*
- *Show the human side: what did the team learn, what surprised them?*
